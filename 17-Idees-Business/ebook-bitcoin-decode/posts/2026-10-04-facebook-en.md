On October 1, 2026, the SEC proposed new custody rules for crypto assets held by investment advisers and regulated funds. The draft would open a form of "self-custody" in narrow cases: only when no qualified custodian is available, and only if the adviser itself holds the keys. Commissioner Hester Peirce noted that this "self-custody" is not the investor's own custody but the adviser holding assets for clients, and said she would have preferred the term "shelf-custody".
