On October 1, 2026, the SEC approved a proposal to amend the custody rules for investment advisers and regulated funds. Since then, most of the argument has been about a single word: self-custody.

Two changes stand out. The proposal widens the list of permitted custodians, adding state-chartered trust companies for crypto assets. It also opens a narrow new option: an adviser may hold client crypto itself, but only after determining that no permitted custodian is available, and that determination has to be made again every quarter.

Commissioner Hester Peirce put quotes around the term on purpose. In her statement, she points out that this "self-custody" is not true self-custody by investors: it describes an adviser acting as custodian for its clients. She says she would have preferred the word "shelf-custody", and she argues regulators should protect the right of owners to hold their own coins.

One detail matters if you hold bitcoin. The proposed amendments apply to crypto assets that are securities or funds, so a private fund holding bitcoin stays largely outside their scope. The rule changes who is allowed to hold your crypto, never who controls the keys.
