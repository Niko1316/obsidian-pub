Worried that omega-3 could affect your heart rhythm? A new study says dose matters.

For years, headlines claimed fish oil increases the risk of atrial fibrillation (AF). A new meta-analysis just clarified the picture.

Published in Circulation: Arrhythmia and Electrophysiology (July 2026), it pooled 35 randomized controlled trials with 114,592 participants, the largest analysis on this question so far, including previously unpublished data.

What it found:
- Low-dose omega-3 (under 1,500 mg/day of EPA+DHA) was NOT associated with a higher AF risk, even in people at elevated cardiovascular risk
- Only high-dose, prescription-strength omega-3 (over 1,500 mg/day) in patients with established heart disease showed a modest increase (0.8% absolute risk)
- In those same high-risk patients, the cardiovascular benefits (fewer heart attacks and strokes) far outweigh that small risk

The takeaway: the dose you actually take matters more than the fear. Most people think they take "a lot" of omega-3, but the label often tells a different story.
