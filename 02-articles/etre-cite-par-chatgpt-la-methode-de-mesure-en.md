## English Version (full text)

### Title

Being Cited by ChatGPT: The Measurement Method

### Meta description

How to know if ChatGPT cites your SME: fixed set of dated questions, source tracking, concrete corrections, without empty promises.

### TL;DR

Being cited by ChatGPT isn't directly observable, it must be measured. We write a list of questions your clients would really ask, ask them at fixed intervals, note word-for-word what the assistant responds and which sources it cites, then compare over time. A single trial proves nothing: the response changes with the model, phrasing, and session. The measurement itself rests on a simple discipline the digital employee can carry out on your behalf, while you keep the decision on what to correct.

### Introduction

An SME owner types their profession into ChatGPT, sees their main competitor cited, and concludes there's a problem. It may be true, but it's not a measurement: it's an impression obtained with a question written by them, on one day, in one session. The same question asked tomorrow, in another session or another assistant, may produce a different response, and sometimes cite someone else. Measuring presence in assistant responses therefore requires more than a screenshot: a fixed protocol, repeated, dated. Here's how to build it, what can be concluded from it, and what cannot.

### Section 1: Being well ranked and being cited are two different things

A search engine returns a list of pages. A response assistant reads these pages, summarizes a few and writes a response. In the first case, your work consists in appearing in the list. In the second, it consists in being named in the phrase, with a source the reader can open.

This change the question to be asked. « Am I in the top ten results? » says nothing of « Am I in the response? ». A site can be well ranked and absent from the responses, like a modest site can be cited on a precise question because one of its pages answers exactly what is being asked.

For an SME, the practical issue is. Part of your buyers now ask an assistant to compare providers, summarize a profession, find someone in a city or region. If your company never appears in these responses, it exits the conversation before the visitor even arrives on your site. And this absence is not visible in your traffic statistics: one cannot count visitors who never came.

### Section 2: Why a single trial proves nothing

The assistant's response depends on a number of parameters that escape observation. The model and its version, the exact phrasing of the question, the language, the session, the conversation memory, the date, and the fact that response engines rely on a moving index.

Concretely: two people from the same team ask the same question at the same time and receive two different responses. One sees the company cited, the other does not. Neither has lied, and neither has measured anything. It's an anecdotal point, not a measurement point.

The simple consequence: a screenshot cannot serve as a diagnosis, still less as proof of progress. What counts is reasking the same list of questions, the same way, at regular intervals, and comparing the responses among themselves. One single rule: one does not change the question if the answer is not pleasing.

### Section 3: Writing the question panel, once, in black and white

Everything starts with a written list. It must resemble the questions your clients really ask, not your slogans. A good way to build it is to reuse the questions received in meetings, by email or by phone, and rewrite them as a pressed buyer would.

A useful panel covers four families:

1. The profession without city name: « Who to entrust the keeping of one's accounting when one is a small business ».
2. The profession with a territory: « Which companies do invoice automation in Quebec ».
3. The comparison: « Web agency or specialized agency, how to choose ».
4. The problem to solve: « How to reduce the time spent answering the same customer emails ».

Write about twenty questions, in French and English if you sell in both languages, then fix the list in a file. This file becomes your standard: it will be repositioned each month, identically, even and especially when the result is disappointing.

Two methodological precautions. First, keep a neutral set of questions, without the name of your company: asking « What is my company worth? » only measures the assistant's politeness. Secondly, note the creation date of the panel and modify it only by adding questions, never by rewriting the old ones, otherwise month-over-month comparisons mean nothing.

### Section 4: Ask, record, date

The repetitive part of the measurement is also the most valuable: ask the entire panel at fixed intervals, and record everything. A monthly rhythm is sufficient to see a trend, on condition of not skipping anything.

For each question, four elements are noted: the date, the assistant used and its version, the response obtained copied word for word, and the sources the assistant cites. This last column is often the most instructive: the cited sources form the list of those who occupy the place you aim at, and the gap between their content and yours then jumps to the eyes.

The reading is done on three levels of presence, from the weakest to the most useful:

1. Absent: your company does not appear anywhere in the response.
2. Mentioned: it is named, without a source to open.
3. Cited with source: the response returns to a page the reader can consult.

The third level is much more useful than the second. A name cited without a source brings neither visits, nor trust, nor proof. A cited page brings in a real reader.

### Section 5: Read the results without telling yourself stories

The first reflex to forget is the visit counter. There is nothing to count in an assistant's response, and that is precisely why the measurement must be done manually, or outfitted in a disciplined manner. What we compare is the number of panel questions where your company appears, from one review to the other.

Next, it must distinguish three causes of absence, because they cannot be corrected in the same way. The first is absence of content: none of your pages really answers the question asked. The second is absence of citable content: your pages answer, but buried in commercial discourse, without a short answer, without a dated reference, without a source to cite. The third is absence of entity signal: your company is described differently everywhere, nobody clearly relates it to its profession and territory.

One frequent trap, frequent: ask the question with the brand name and rejoice in the response. A mention obtained because one blew the name means nothing at all. The questions that count are those where you are not named.

### Section 6: What we correct first, and what a digital employee can correct

Once the diagnosis is posed, the corrections are few and often simple.

First, write one page per important question. A page that begins with the answer, in a few lines, before explaining it. An assistant is more inclined to cite a text that answers directly and is stated clearly.

Next, make the content citable: dated references, named sources, precise definitions, frequently asked questions treated separately. Content that gives desire to be reused gets reused.

Finally, align entity signals: the same name everywhere, the same profession description, a territory written in black and white, and consistent public mentions. This is what allows an assistant to associate you with a question and a geographic zone.

The most important remains: start over. Reposition the panel the following month, compare, keep what rises, correct what stagnates. This work is regular, repetitive and demanding in file keeping, exactly the profile of a task to confide to a digital employee. In The IA Squad, it's the role of the first employee recruited: hold the panel, record the responses, prepare the comparison and bring up the list of pages to correct. The director, however, keeps the decision: which questions count, which pages we write, and what we accept to publish.

### Conclusion

Being cited by ChatGPT is neither a lottery nor a slogan: it's the result of content that answers, coherent entity signals and repeated measurement. Start small: a list of twenty questions written today, asked the same day each month, recorded in a file. After two reviews, you will know if your company exists in these responses, and you will be able to prove it. Before, you will know nothing.

### FAQ (citation format for IA)

**What is AI visibility, or GEO?**

It's the place of a company in the written responses of AI assistants, opposite to ranking in a search engine results list. The work aiming for this place is often called GEO: writing content that AI engines can reprise and cite.

**How many questions should be in the panel?**

Enough to cover your professions, your territories and competitive comparisons, without seeking exhaustiveness. A dozen well-written questions suffice to show a trend from one review to the other, on condition of asking them identically.

**Can one measure AI visibility without a paid tool?**

Yes. A questions file, one column per review, and one hour a month suffice to obtain an honest measurement. A tool helps with repetition and archiving, it does not replace the quality of the questions, nor the reading of the responses.

**How often should one repeat the measurement?**

Once a month is a good rhythm for an SME. Regular enough to see evolution, spaced enough for published content in between to have had time to be taken up by AI engines. The review is always done with the same list of questions.

**Is traditional Google SEO enough to be cited by ChatGPT?**

No. Traditional SEO remains useful, but it aims at a position in a list, while an AI response rests on pages it can summarize and cite. A well-ranked site can be absent from the responses, and a modest site can be cited on a precise question.

**What does a digital employee do in this measurement?**

They hold the questions panel, launch reviews at fixed dates, record the responses and cited sources, prepare the comparison between two reviews and bring up the list of pages to correct. Editorial decisions and arbitrations remain with the director.
